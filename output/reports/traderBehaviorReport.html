Hyperliquid Trader Behavior

Static export of the interview-narrative dashboard — charts stay interactive, no server required. Full evidence and caveats: docs/findings.md in the repo.

1. Overview

40,471 leaderboard addresses, a stratified sample of 302 with fills history (2.08M fills), analyzed through four statusTier segments: activeCore, activeLongTail, churnedTrader, silentHolder.

2. A data-quality catch: the 10k fill cap

Hyperliquid's userFillsByTime endpoint hard-caps at 10,000 most recent fills per address (server-side, not a pagination bug). 67/302 sampled addresses (22.2%) hit it, which silently understated activeSpanDays for the affected addresses before correction.

3. Why traders churn

Liquidation, a single catastrophic trade, and position-escalation-into-blowup were all ruled out first. shareNetProfitable is flat and non-monotonic across the four months leading up to each account's last fill (slope ≈ 0.0022) — churn is disengagement-driven, not preceded by a decline in trading edge.

4. Why activeLongTail underperforms

Fee burden was ruled out first (flat ~0.03% of notional across every tier). activeLongTail sizes positions ~24-90x more aggressively relative to account equity than activeCore, and relies on taker (aggressive) execution more (92.2% vs. 81.7% of fills) — and that taker execution performs worse for them specifically.

5. Clustering: trading style vs. account status

KMeans (k=4) vs. statusTier gives an adjusted Rand index of 0.079; HDBSCAN gives 0.010 — both near the 0 expected under random labeling. statusTier is built from account state (equity scale, recency); this feature space captures trading style (intensity, execution aggressiveness). The two are close to orthogonal.

6. Within churnedTrader: two different stories

Re-clustering churnedTrader alone (no tier labels) splits it roughly 55/45: one group disengaged despite a strong taker win rate (61.5%), the other has a 100% taker share and the worst taker win rate found anywhere in the project (41.2%) — independently rediscovering the activeLongTail mechanism.
